# Genesis Engine Social Organization, Territory, and Conflict Skill

## Purpose

This skill helps AI assistants design and review Genesis Engine systems involving:

- emergent grouping and affiliation;
- kin-biased behavior;
- cooperation and resource exchange;
- social memory and recognition;
- territorial occupancy and defense;
- coalition formation;
- organized inter-group conflict;
- social learning and traditions.

The goal is not to implement societies. The goal is to create physical and ecological conditions from which social patterns may emerge.

## Activation

Use this skill when a task involves:

- adding or modifying social mechanics;
- designing world affordances for cooperation or conflict;
- evaluating claims that organisms formed groups, cultures, territories, or coalitions;
- planning experiments about emergent behavior;
- reviewing architecture for hidden social assumptions.

## Core Genesis Principles

Always preserve:

1. Author physics and affordances, not progress.
2. Emergence must come from mechanisms, not labels.
3. Observer classifications must never influence organisms.
4. No LLM, scripted faction system, or external intelligence controls organisms.
5. Determinism is part of the scientific method.

Never add direct inputs such as:

- group_id;
- faction membership;
- territory owner;
- reputation score;
- ally/enemy flags;
- war state;
- cultural labels.

Use physical mechanisms instead:

- perception;
- movement;
- resources;
- memory;
- signals;
- objects;
- structures;
- injury;
- consequences.

## Design Guidance

### Group formation

Prefer this causal chain:

embodied perception → repeated encounters → familiarity/assortment → persistent associations → observer-inferred communities

Do not begin with groups. A group should be an analysis result.

### Recognition

Preferred mechanisms:

- familiarity;
- perceptible phenotype cues;
- spatial association;
- learned individual fingerprints;
- locally evolved signals.

Avoid direct genetic relatedness access. Pedigree may exist for analysis only.

### Cooperation

Implement:

- carrying;
- dropping;
- transfer;
- joint manipulation;
- shared structures;
- environmental modification.

Do not implement:

- cooperate();
- share_with_ally();
- help_group();

Measure costs and benefits experimentally.

### Territory

Represent:

- resources;
- structures;
- markers;
- occupancy;
- spatial memory;
- defensive actions.

Do not represent:

- owned polygons;
- faction borders;
- territory IDs.

A territory is an observer conclusion requiring repeated use and active defense.

### Conflict

Damage is an affordance, not a social explanation.

A valid progression is:

individual aggression → group-correlated aggression → coalitionary coordination → repeated organized conflict

Do not infer war from:

- attacks alone;
- simultaneous attackers;
- resource scarcity alone;
- kin clustering alone.

Coordination requires evidence above independent common-stimulus explanations.

## Experimental Requirements

Every major social claim should include:

### Controls

- opportunity-matched null models;
- alternative ecological explanations;
- positive controls for capability;
- observer-label removal tests.

### Ablations

Examples:

- remove memory;
- remove signals;
- scramble cues;
- disable structures;
- flatten resource concentration;
- remove damage;
- disable social learning.

### Measurements

Prefer:

- event-based metrics;
- seed-level replication;
- causal comparisons;
- confidence intervals;
- explicit uncertainty.

Do not rely on:

- one impressive run;
- visual interpretation;
- post hoc labels.

## Determinism Checklist

For every mechanism ask:

- What authoritative state does it add?
- How is it serialized?
- What phase updates it?
- What ordering rules apply?
- What RNG namespace is used?
- Can observer analysis alter it?
- Does replay remain identical?

Common hazards:

- hash-map iteration;
- nondeterministic neighbor ordering;
- floating-point divergence;
- global RNG streams;
- ambiguous simultaneous actions.

## Common Anti-Patterns

Reject designs that:

- create tribes before interaction exists;
- assign enemies;
- reward killing directly;
- expose observer classifications;
- create magic communication channels;
- use unlimited memory;
- confuse proximity with affiliation;
- confuse labels with mechanisms.

## Decision Framework

Before adding a social feature:

1. What physical problem does it solve?
2. Could the behavior emerge without this primitive?
3. What cheaper mechanism should be tested first?
4. What experiment distinguishes alternatives?
5. What ablation would falsify this mechanism?
6. How is the result measured?

## Limitations

This skill does not prove that Genesis will evolve:

- cooperation;
- culture;
- technology;
- territoriality;
- warfare;
- human-like societies.

The research summarizes mechanisms and proposes engineering approaches. Some recommendations are engineering judgments rather than scientific consensus.

Important uncertainties include:

- open-ended evolution remains unsolved;
- artificial systems may exploit unintended shortcuts;
- social categories are observer-dependent;
- biological evidence does not guarantee artificial emergence;
- rare evolutionary events require extensive replication.

All major Genesis conclusions require validation through controlled deterministic experiments.
