# Genesis Cumulative Culture Skill

## Purpose

Use this skill when working on Genesis Engine systems involving:

- social learning
- cultural transmission
- traditions
- cumulative improvement
- artifact-mediated knowledge
- persistent structures
- emergent technology
- population-level behavioral evolution
- experimental validation of cultural claims

This skill converts research on cultural evolution into engineering guidance. It does not provide a technology roadmap or prescribe civilization stages.

## Activation

Apply this skill when an AI assistant is asked to:

- design new Genesis Engine mechanisms related to culture or technology;
- review architecture proposals;
- evaluate whether a simulation result demonstrates culture;
- design experiments, metrics, ablations, or benchmarks;
- add perception, learning, artifacts, communication, or social systems;
- investigate failures of emergent behavior.

## Core Genesis Principles

Always preserve:

1. Author physics and affordances, not progress.
2. Emergence must result from mechanisms and selection, not scripted outcomes.
3. Offline analysis observes; it never modifies organism behavior.
4. Organisms must never receive hidden semantic labels such as "tool", "technology", "teacher", or "era".
5. Do not use LLMs as organism controllers.
6. Determinism is a scientific requirement:
   - random streams must be explicit and reproducible;
   - ordering must not depend on containers or threads;
   - learned state must serialize;
   - checkpoints must restore exactly.
7. Claims must be measurable and tested against alternatives.

## Scientific Boundaries

Do not treat the following as evidence of cumulative culture by themselves:

- increasing complexity;
- visually impressive structures;
- tool use;
- strategy changes;
- larger populations;
- longer action sequences;
- higher neural complexity;
- single successful runs.

A valid cumulative-culture claim requires:

1. a variant appears;
2. the variant is socially or artifact mediated;
3. it persists beyond the origin event;
4. it improves a relevant function;
5. later variants retain and depend on earlier variants.

## Design Guidance

### Social learning

Prefer ordinary perception over privileged channels.

Good:
- seeing another organism move;
- seeing object-state changes;
- seeing consequences;
- learning from artifacts.

Avoid:
- action IDs;
- hidden reward transfer;
- copied neural weights;
- direct skill transfer.

Test different mechanisms separately:

- local enhancement;
- stimulus enhancement;
- outcome learning/emulation;
- action-form learning;
- learned signals;
- artifact-mediated learning.

## Lifetime Learning

Culture requires acquired state.

Recommended architecture:

- genetically inherited controller;
- bounded lifetime plasticity;
- deterministic learning updates;
- explicit memory limits;
- complete learned-state checkpointing.

Avoid unlimited memory or perfect replay systems that become hidden cultural archives.

## Artifacts and Technology

Implement generic physical possibilities:

- carry;
- place;
- push;
- pull;
- strike;
- deform;
- combine;
- separate;
- modify.

Do not implement:

- recipes;
- crafting trees;
- technology unlocks;
- named tools;
- progress scores.

Artifacts should become culturally meaningful only if later organisms learn from historically produced physical state.

## Experimental Framework

When proposing experiments include:

- hypothesis;
- mechanism tested;
- controls;
- ablations;
- independent variables;
- dependent variables;
- seed requirements;
- deterministic replay requirements;
- failure interpretation.

Recommended progression:

1. deterministic instrumentation;
2. social diffusion of arbitrary variants;
3. persistence after inventor death;
4. competition between variants;
5. one retained improvement;
6. multiple dependent improvements;
7. group divergence;
8. artifact-mediated learning;
9. signals and teaching-like behavior;
10. weakly specified open ecology.

Do not skip dependencies.

## Review Checklist

Before approving a design, ask:

### Mechanism
- What physical pathway creates the behavior?
- Could the behavior happen without the proposed cultural mechanism?
- Is information transferred through normal perception?

### Emergence
- Is the system defining possibilities or defining outcomes?
- Is there hidden progress logic?
- Are categories only offline?

### Scientific validity
- What is the null hypothesis?
- What ablation removes the claimed mechanism?
- Could genetic evolution explain the result?
- Could independent rediscovery explain the result?

### Determinism
- Are RNG streams explicit?
- Are updates order-independent?
- Is all state checkpointed?
- Are analytics isolated?

### Measurements
- Is transmission measured?
- Is persistence measured?
- Is lineage reconstructed?
- Is causal dependence demonstrated?

## Common Anti-Patterns

Reject:

- "add a technology tree but hide it";
- "reward inventions";
- "give smarter organisms better APIs";
- "copy expert neural networks";
- "classify behavior online and reward it";
- "increase complexity and call it culture";
- "one amazing run proves emergence";
- "make progress easier because it is the goal."

## Limitations

This research does not prove that open-ended technology will emerge.

It supports mechanisms that can enable:

- traditions;
- social learning;
- bounded cumulative improvement;
- artifact-mediated transmission.

Evidence remains weak for:

- indefinite technological expansion;
- civilization-like transitions;
- language emergence;
- institutions;
- organized conflict;
- human-scale cultural scope.

Many recommendations are engineering judgments derived from literature rather than settled scientific laws.

All major Genesis claims require validation through controlled experiments.
