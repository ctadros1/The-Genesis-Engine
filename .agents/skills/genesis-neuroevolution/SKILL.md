# Genesis Neuroevolution Skill

## Activation

Use this skill when working on Genesis Engine systems involving:

- neural controller architecture;
- evolving topology, weights, or learning rules;
- recurrent networks;
- synaptic plasticity;
- controller genomes;
- crossover and mutation;
- checkpointing learned state;
- experiments involving adaptation, learning, or behavioral complexity.

## Purpose

Help AI assistants make engineering decisions that preserve Genesis Engine's scientific goals:

- author physics and affordances, not progress;
- allow behavior to emerge from mechanisms;
- keep analysis systems observational only;
- never use LLMs as organism controllers;
- treat determinism, replay, and serialization as core architecture requirements.

## Core Guidance

### Prefer staged complexity

Do not combine topology evolution, lifetime learning, developmental growth, social learning, and new persistence formats in one change.

Recommended order:

1. deterministic graph representation;
2. recurrent evaluation;
3. structural mutation;
4. crossover and innovation handling;
5. exact runtime persistence;
6. bounded lifetime plasticity;
7. evolved plasticity parameters;
8. modularity mechanisms;
9. indirect/developmental research branches.

### Default architecture recommendation

The preferred production direction is:

- direct graph genome;
- NEAT-inspired historical alignment concepts;
- deterministic structural identities;
- explicit recurrent delays;
- bounded local plasticity rules;
- exact save/restore of learned state.

Do not assume classic NEAT implementation details are mandatory. Preserve the mechanisms that solve problems:
- structural alignment;
- innovation protection;
- incremental mutation;
- complexity control.

## Design Rules

### Determinism

Every recommendation must consider:

- named keyed RNG streams;
- canonical ordering;
- stable IDs;
- serialization order;
- replay compatibility;
- numeric policy;
- versioning.

Never rely on:

- hash map iteration;
- thread scheduling;
- random traversal order;
- platform-dependent floating behavior.

### Genome design

Separate:

- gene lineage identity;
- crossover homology identity;
- structural signature;
- mutation event identity.

Do not collapse ancestry and phenotype equivalence into one identifier.

### Structural evolution

Support:

- add connection;
- split connection/add node;
- enable/disable;
- deletion;
- pruning.

Avoid add-only topology growth. Long-running systems require simplification mechanisms.

### Lifetime learning

Prefer bounded local learning rules first:

- Hebbian variants;
- Oja-like normalization;
- three-factor rules with eligibility traces and modulators.

Avoid starting with:

- arbitrary evolved learning programs;
- lifetime structural plasticity;
- Lamarckian inheritance.

## Review Framework

When reviewing a proposal ask:

1. What mechanism creates the behavior?
2. Is the mechanism observable and testable?
3. Does it introduce hidden objectives?
4. Is all state exactly serializable?
5. Can the result be replayed identically?
6. Does the proposal scale to thousands of organisms?
7. What ablation would prove the mechanism matters?

## Common Anti-Patterns

Reject or question:

- "This will create intelligence/culture automatically."
- "More complex networks are always better."
- "A deterministic seed is enough for determinism."
- "Learned behavior can be reconstructed from the genome."
- "Modularity metrics alone prove functional modules."
- "A behavioral tradition proves social learning."
- "A compact genome means a cheap simulation."
- "NEAT always beats fixed networks."

## Experimental Requirements

Claims should be measurable.

Prefer:

- paired seeds;
- ablations;
- fixed baselines;
- operation-count accounting;
- replay checksums;
- newborn versus experienced comparisons;
- learned-state reset experiments;
- cross-fostering and transmission controls for social learning.

## Limitations

This research does not prove that open-ended complexity, culture, intelligence, or technological behavior will emerge.

Evidence is strongest for individual mechanisms:
- neuroevolution methods;
- recurrent control;
- local plasticity;
- modularity under certain pressures;
- deterministic engineering practices.

Evidence is weaker for:
- combining all mechanisms in one persistent artificial ecosystem;
- developmental encodings at Genesis scale;
- lifelong structural plasticity;
- gene-culture evolution.

Many recommendations are engineering judgments designed around Genesis constraints. They require validation through controlled experiments.
