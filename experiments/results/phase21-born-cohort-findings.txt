The question
------------
Phase 20 (D-134) ended with the rate-limiting term of every lineage: a
born organism lives a median ~268 ticks against a materialized one's
~1,784 with a comparable start, so one born organism in fourteen
reproduces before it starves. Why does a born organism live a sixth as
long?

Two facts of the tick, settled by tests before any campaign
-----------------------------------------------------------
1. Where it starts. A child is placed within 2 m of its parents'
   midpoint in 4 m cells - the cell its parents are eating in, or the
   one beside it.
2. When it eats. Both feeding passes visit organisms in entity-ID order
   and a cell's biomass and substrate are taken in that order, so a
   newborn - the highest ID in the world - eats after every older
   occupant of its cell. The youngest eats last. Pinned by
   crates/sim-core/tests/phase21_cohort.rs: an elder (ID 1, aged 30)
   and a newcomer (ID 2, aged 0) in one cell, both hungry; the elder
   takes planted substrate first and planted biomass first; reversing
   either pair of feeding loops fails its clause. Nothing was written to
   make it so; it is what an ordered loop over a shared cell does.

The instrument
--------------
BirthSite (event tag 31, schema 13, 67 bytes): at every admission -
births and materializations alike - the cell, the organisms already in
it (every one ahead of the newcomer in the order), the organism's own
trait-derived maturity, and the cell's four substrates, microbial
density and biomass as the newcomer finds them. The census (lifesim
cohort, lifesim-cohort-index-v1) reads it per cohort: completed
lifespans, food at birth (primordial + monomer + biomass; waste,
polymer and microbial density apart), occupants, block-stratified rank
correlations (20,000-tick admission blocks, so the run's own trend
from empty ground to a grazed crowd cannot pose as place or order),
maturity and reproduction by food quartile and occupancy. The record
moves no checksum (verify-phase21, six clauses).

The first pilot: the shipped world, four seeds
----------------------------------------------
The median born organism starts in a cell with ZERO food (waste ~10,500
and microbial ~15,500 milli, no primordial, monomer or biomass) against
~12,650 for a materialized one; it has one occupant beside it. Its
completed lifespan correlates with occupants at birth (median block rho
-306, range -344..-281) more strongly than with food (274, range
256..297), and the within-block partials sharpen it (occupants -219
after food; food 135 after occupants). With two or more occupants at
birth almost no born organism reaches maturity (per world ~893 / 1,152
/ 136 / 3 at 0 / 1 / 2 / 3+). The licence rule written before the
pilot - occupants at least as strong as food - was met in every world,
so the probe was built: lifesim-intake-order-v2, both feeding passes
visiting organisms in descending index order (youngest first, since
age rank and ID rank coincide here), a permutation of who takes first
and nothing else, hashed only when selected, ALIF format 16.

The second pilot: the same seeds under both orders
--------------------------------------------------
| seed | O1 born median | O2 born median | delta | O1 mat | O2 mat |
|------|----------------|----------------|-------|--------|--------|
| 21901 | 244 | 2,171 | +1,927 | 1,762 | 1,205 |
| 21902 | 192 | 2,150 | +1,958 | 1,709 | 1,176 |
| 21903 | 191 | 2,037 | +1,846 | 1,959 | 1,227 |
| 21904 | 228 | 2,597 | +2,369 | 1,702 | 1,170 |

Youngest-first lifts the born cohort's median life tenfold, above the
materialized cohort's, and lowers the materialized median by ~545: what
the youngest now take, the eldest no longer get. Born organisms
reaching their own maturity go from ~1,600 to ~9,600 per world, and
reproducing from ~880 to ~8,250. Both associations vanish under O2
(food rho median 13; occupants 14.5). The plan's stated expectation -
that place carries most of it - was wrong: the order is the term, and
place and company were its shadow, because the cell a newborn is born
into is grazed, in that same tick, by the organisms that eat before it.
The pre-registration records the revision with its reason and is
locked on the probe shape: SESOI 971 ticks (half the median pair
difference, by a rule fixed before the pilot), bar 22 of 30.

Traps met and named
-------------------
- An expectation stated in advance can be wrong; the record says so
  and why, and the SESOI came from a rule fixed before the data, not
  from the surprise.
- "Contemporaries", "food" and "the second module's type" each needed a
  definition the data could compute; the adversarial review supplied
  them before the build.
- The occupant count must be built at the start of lifecycle from the
  tick's positions; the spatial buckets are pre-movement.
- A materialized organism's record shows the density it condensed
  from, its own debit landing after the record; a test that demanded
  equality was wrong, not the record.
- A one-pass check over a tick's events classified a record before its
  organism's admission record arrived; two passes.
