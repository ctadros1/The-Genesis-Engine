======================================================
Recorded 2026-09-03. ADR-0035; pre-registration
experiments/phase20-lineage-preregistration.md LOCKED before any
confirmatory world ran. Reduction:
experiments/results/phase20-lineage-reduction.py (counts, decides

The question
------------
Phase 19 (D-133) ended with bodies above one module appearing in 17 of
30 worlds under coupling v2, one organism at a time by the 500-tick
series, and none by the horizon: no multi-module lineage. Why?

What was settled by arithmetic and by tests before any campaign
---------------------------------------------------------------
1. Price is not the reason. The phenotype's basal multiplier is the
   body's summed upkeep relative to the founder body's (mass 2,400,
   upkeep 750, intake 1,000) clamped to [0.6, 1.6]; the unicell (800 /
   200 / 1,000) sits on the floor at 0.6, and a second module of five of
   the seven types - structural 300, sensory 350, digestive 400,
   storage 280, reproductive 450 - leaves it there while conferring
   capability (a second gut: intake x1.2; storage: capacity 12,000 ->
   60,000; a motor: speed 500 -> 2,116). Only a motor (0.8) or a neural
   module (0.93) lifts the multiplier, by at most ~3 milli per tick
   against ~200 of intake. Pinned by
   crates/sim-core/tests/phase20_lineage.rs. The plan's first draft had
   proposed a uniform upkeep-scale knob; it was withdrawn before it was
   built (ADR-0035 records why).
2. Transmission is Mendelian and nothing refuses the carrier
   (crates/sim-core/tests/phase20_reproduction.rs, the kernel's own
   recombine-mutate-develop sequence with keyed draws): a two-module
   genome found from the unicell at keyed draw 6,398 (a duplicated gut;
   the unicell x unicell cross yields a second module in 0 of 1,000
   draws) reproduces against a unicell into 488 of 1,000 two-module
   children and against itself into 767 of 1,000; 0 refused as
   non-viable, 0-2 on node budget; its compatibility distance to the
   unicell is 0.0000 against the 0.5 pairing threshold.
3. There was no body record to read. GrowthCompleted (Phase 14) is
   emitted only on the ontogeny path and every Phase 16 and 19 campaign
   ran with ontogeny off, so the census over Phase 19's own logs is
   empty by construction. The BodyComposition record (event tag 30,
   schema 12) is emitted at every admission and the census keys on it.

The pilot's reading (four worlds, 100,000 ticks, the shipped world)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
17 multi-module organisms across the four worlds, every one of them
born (a materialized organism always starts from the constant one-
module map), 16 a doubled gut and one gut-plus-motor: the duplication
that survives development is overwhelmingly a copy of the gut's own
regulatory locus. None of the 17 reproduced; none had offspring; second
generation 0. Their completed lifespans (per-world medians 191, 218,
261, 133 ticks) match their matched one-module cohort's (259, 238, 144,
140; median difference -13.5 ticks): the second module costs nothing
visible in life length, as the arithmetic said.

What the census then showed about the whole born cohort is the finding
that reframes the question. Born organisms do reproduce - 1,803, 1,303,
834 and 822 distinct born parents per world, 72 percent of births
having a born parent - but each one rarely (born parents / births =
8.3 percent pooled), because the world-wide born median lifespan (293,
235, 220, 210 ticks) sits below the trait-derived maturity's floor of
400. The ecology is a long-lived materialized parent stock (~1,400-tick
lives, D-133) feeding a short-lived born population, and a second
module only ever appears in the short-lived half. The pairing gate's
other preconditions refused nobody: energy 0, capacity 0, placement 0-1
in every world (the ~900-1,200 "nonviable" pair rejections per world
are all node-budget refusals, C10.7's brain-costs-body coupling, ~7
percent of births). The decision tree's Branch A applied by its letter
(no multi-module parent) but its lever - the pairing energy gate - was
premised on a refusal that never happens, so the two-arm contrast was
not run and its campaign file was removed with the record.

Appearances: 17 in 57,315 births = 2.97 per 10,000 - about eight times
the Phase 19 series-based count, which sampled every 500 ticks and
missed most ~200-tick lives. "One at a time" was a sampling artefact
of the instrument, not a fact about the organisms.

The cohort prediction, stated before the confirmatory: appearances per
birth x born-parent fraction x offspring per born parent x one half =
1.016 second-generation organisms per 10,000 births, 5.8 expected over
the pilot's births against 0 observed (Poisson ~0.003) - a deficit the
four worlds could not settle and the fifty were run to.

Traps met and named
-------------------
- A criterion on a 500-tick series peak under-counts lives shorter than
  the interval by an order of magnitude; the event log is the instrument
  for anything that lives for hundreds of ticks.
- A "price" argument in registry upkeep units is not an argument about
  what the world charges; the clamps decide, and they are pinned now.
- A decision tree's lever must be premised on an observable refusal;
  when the counters say the gate refuses nobody, the branch's letter is
  not a licence to run its arm.
- A parser arm without its index advance hangs the fixture; no pin sees
  it, the run does.
