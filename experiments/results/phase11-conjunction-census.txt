0. THE CONJUNCTION, CONFIRMED FROM THE CODE, WITH TWO CORRECTIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------

The reading in the request is right about the shape and needs two
amendments. A nonzero learned delta on one edge needs, on the same locus:

  1. `EDGE_FLAG_PLASTIC` set
     point-mutation target 1, a toggle          structmut.rs:675
     only a flagged edge enters `plastic_edges`  controller2.rs:236
     expression ORs the two alleles' flags       genome2.rs:1570

  2. `rule_id != RULE_STATIC`
     point-mutation target 2, a re-draw          structmut.rs:676, :633
     rule 0 returns `StepKind::Static` with the state unchanged, before
     eta, the coefficients, the decay or the modulator are read at all
                                                 plasticity.rs:375-377

  3. `eta > 0`
     point-mutation target 3, `+delta` clamped at 0 from below
                                                 structmut.rs:677
     every rule form multiplies by eta:
       rules 1 and 2 `raw * eta`                 plasticity.rs:427
       rule 3 `raw * eta * modulator`            plasticity.rs:425
       rule 4 `to_q16_checked(raw * eta)` into the trace
                                                 plasticity.rs:402

  4. a nonzero coefficient - EXCEPT UNDER RULE_OJA. **Confirmed.**
     point-mutation target 4                     structmut.rs:679-681
     `RULE_OJA => post * (pre - post * w_eff)` reads no coefficient
                                                 plasticity.rs:388
     every other rule goes through `hebbian`, which is
     `c0*x*y + c1*x + c2*y + c3` and is identically zero when all four
     coefficients are zero                       plasticity.rs:347, :391
     pinned in sim-core by
     `oja_normalizes_toward_the_effective_weight_and_ignores_the_coefficients`

  CORRECTION A - eta is required for Oja too. Oja is exempt from the
  coefficient condition only. `delta = raw * eta` applies to rule 2 exactly
  as to rule 1 (plasticity.rs:427), so an Oja edge needs conditions 1, 2
  and 3, not two.

  CORRECTION B - THERE IS A FIFTH CONDITION, and the four-target reading
  misses it in the opposite direction. Rules 3 and 4 multiply by the
  modulator activation, and `learn_phase` hands a hard `0.0` to any edge
  whose modulator did not resolve (world.rs:3953-3957). Resolution needs
  `modulator_node != 0` AND that node's role to be `Modulatory`
  (controller2.rs:253-264) - a seventh mutation target (structmut.rs:683)
  and a *node*-arm role re-draw on a different locus (structmut.rs:604).
  So a modulated edge with no usable modulator computes zero forever
  whatever else it carries. It is reported separately, as
  `full_conjunction_gated`, rather than folded into the depth histogram,
  which is kept at the four conditions the request asked for.

  ONE MORE, reported and not counted: diploid expression can assemble the
  conjunction ACROSS haplotypes even when neither allele carries it.
  `blend_plasticity` (genome2.rs:1358-1395) takes `rule_id` and
  `modulator_node` from haplotype 0 and blends `eta` and the coefficients
  by dominance, and the flag is the OR. That is why the expressed count
  below is larger than the allele count, and it is the mechanism by which
  the phenotype appeared at all.

1. THE HEADLINE
----------------------------------------------------------------

**The null is about reachability, and the decisive number is 9.**

Across the 30 Avar worlds, 9 of 684,370 edge alleles satisfy all four
conditions - 13 per million. 3 of those 9 also have a named modulator where
the rule needs one. The other 684,361 alleles carry a genotype on which the
learn phase is arithmetically incapable of moving a weight.

**But it is not a clean zero, and the second half of the finding is that
the phenotype WAS assembled, rarely, and did almost nothing when it was.**
Diploid expression is more permissive than either allele: 59 of the 48,119
expressed plastic edges in Avar clear the four conditions (1,226 per
million), 29 of them with a resolvable modulator, spread over 17 of the 30
worlds. And 25 plastic-edge rows out of 48,119 hold a nonzero learned value
at tick 60,000, in 14 of the 30 worlds.

So the campaign's central claim needs one correction. The findings file
says the final mean absolute learned delta was 0 milli in all 30 Avar
worlds and reads that as "the mechanism moved no weight". The mean is 0,
and the mean is a rounding artefact: the total absolute learned magnitude
in the Avar arm is 139,116 Q16 = 2.1227 weight units, and
`2.1227 * 1000 / 48119` truncates to 0 milli. Twenty-five edges did learn.
The largest learned delta anywhere in the arm is 15,054 Q16 = 0.2297 weight
units = 229 milli, on a weight bounded at 8.

That is the corrected reading: **not "learning was measured and did
nothing", and not "learning never happened", but "the learning phenotype
was assembled about sixty times out of forty-eight thousand plastic edges,
moved twenty-five of them, and the population statistic could not have
resolved that if it had mattered."** The 25 nonzero Avar learned values run
from 11 Q16 (0.00017 weight units) to 15,054 Q16 (0.2297), with a median of 2,930
Q16 = 0.045 weight units = 45 milli. That median is reconstructed from the
per-world quantiles and sums in `phase11-conjunction-census-raw.txt`; the
per-world lines are the primary record.

The supporting number, which changes what "a billion updates" means:

  Of the 48,119 edges expressed as plastic in Avar, **44,972 (93.46%) carry
  rule 0**, and rule 0 is a complete no-op. Of the 1,109,373,897 learn-phase
  calls the findings file quotes, **1,058,712,033 (95.43%) returned
  `StepKind::Static` having read and written nothing**; only 50,661,864
  (4.57%) evaluated a rule form. `plasticity_updates_total` is
  `PlasticityCounters::total_evaluated`, which is applied + static +
  refused, so the headline counts the no-ops.

  Zero clamp saturations and zero trace saturations in 1.1 billion calls.
  `PlasticityCounters::total_saturations`'s own documentation says a long
  run should saturate and "a zero here would suggest nothing was learning".
  It is zero. Zero faults as well, in both A arms.

3. IS THERE A SELECTIVE GRADIENT ANYWHERE ON THE PATH?
----------------------------------------------------------------

**No positive one anywhere, and a negative one on every state that carries
the flag. The interior of the path is a plateau with a moat around the
half of it that costs energy.** Argued from the code, then measured.

3a. EVERY INCOMPLETE STATE COMPUTES BIT-IDENTICALLY TO THE FOUNDER.

  `plasticity::step` returns at its first branch for rule 0
  (plasticity.rs:375-377) - `StepOutcome::unchanged(state, Static, false)`,
  before eta, the coefficients, the decay or the modulator are read. So an
  edge with `eta = 0.9` and `rule_id = 0` and an edge with `eta = 0.0` and
  `rule_id = 0` execute the same instructions and write the same bytes.
  Pinned by `static_rule_is_a_complete_no_op` in sim-core, which sets a full
  decay and a saturating learned value and asserts the state is untouched,
  with a rule-1 control on the same parameters that does move.

  With `rule_id != 0` and `eta == 0`: `delta = raw * 0.0 = 0.0`,
  `to_q16(0.0) = 0` (plasticity.rs:433), `decay_toward_zero(0, d) = 0`,
  `accumulate_clamped(0, 0) = (0, false)`. Learned state stays 0.

  With `rule_id != 0`, `eta > 0` and all coefficients zero under rules 1, 3
  or 4: `hebbian([0,0,0,0], x, y) = 0.0` exactly (plasticity.rs:347), and
  the same chain gives 0.

  A learned value of 0 makes the edge's evaluation identical to a
  non-plastic edge's. `effective_weight(w, 0) = (w + 0.0).clamp(-8, 8)`
  (plasticity.rs:336-338), and every genome weight is already bounded to
  `VALUE_LIMIT = 8` at decode, so the clamp is the identity and the
  multiplier at the summation site is bit-for-bit `edge.weight` -
  controller2.rs:540-547, whose own comment states it.

  Without the flag, none of conditions 2, 3 or 4 is even reachable by the
  runtime: `compile_with_budget` lists only `edge.plastic`
  (controller2.rs:236), so the edge is compiled as an ordinary fixed edge,
  the learn phase never visits it, and it pays nothing.

  So: **every state on the path except the complete one is behaviourally
  invisible.** Selection has nothing to act on. Drift is the only force.

3b. THE PLASTICITY ENERGY COST DOES NOT RESCUE THE GRADIENT. IT MAKES HALF
    THE PATH DELETERIOUS.

  `learn_phase` charges `plan.plastic_edges.len() * cost_per_edge_thousandths`
  (world.rs:4008-4010) - the count of plastic edges, with no reference to
  the rule. The comment above it is explicit: "Every plastic edge pays,
  including a rule-0 edge that writes nothing ... charging only edges that
  moved would make 'turn the rule off' a free way to keep the flag."

  At this campaign's `plastic_edge_cost_milli_per_s = 2` and `dt_ms = 100`
  (world.rs:906-907), the charge is 200 thousandths = **0.2 milli-EU per
  plastic edge per tick**, exact to within one milli because D-094 made the
  remainder carry. Basal metabolism is 100 milli/s = 10 milli/tick per
  organism, so one flagged edge is **2% of an organism's basal burn**, paid
  every tick of its life, in a world where energy is what kills: the first
  Avar world records 80,906 starvation deaths against 88,439 births.

  Measured, not asserted: the Avar arm paid **221,410,876 milli-EU** for
  plastic edges. The predicted charge is 1,109,373,897 edge-ticks x 0.2 =
  221,874,779 milli, so the ledger is 99.79% of the exact price - the
  shortfall is organisms that died owing it. **95.43% of that, about
  211,300,000 milli-EU, was charged for edges running rule 0**: machinery
  that returned at the first branch without reading a gene.

  So the sign of every step is:

    conditions 2, 3, 4 without the flag   exactly neutral (never compiled)
    the flag alone                         deleterious, 0.2 milli/tick, no
                                           computational difference at all
    the flag + any incomplete subset       deleterious by the same amount,
                                           still no computational difference
    the complete conjunction               same cost, and the only state in
                                           which the arithmetic can do
                                           anything at all

  That is a fitness valley of depth one metabolic charge and width three
  mutational steps, with a plateau on the cheap side of it. Evolution
  cannot climb this: there is no monotone path of non-negative steps from
  the founder to the phenotype. **The C11.2 null - "plasticity spread at
  the drift rate and not above it" - is exactly what this landscape
  predicts, and it is a statement about the landscape rather than about the
  value of learning.**

  The one caveat that keeps this honest: the flag is genuinely deleterious
  and the arm still carries it on 7.98% of edge alleles, which is the same
  neighbourhood C11.2 measured its neutral marker in (6.1 percent of marker
  alleles in the median Avar world, on that criterion's per-organism scale -
  not the same statistic as this one and not directly differenced here). At
  a per-edge selection coefficient this small, over this many generations,
  drift dominates and the cost is not resolvable in this data. The cost is
  enough to forbid a positive gradient; it is not enough to have visibly
  purged the flag.

4. EXPECTED WAITING TIME FOR THE CONJUNCTION AT ONE LOCUS
----------------------------------------------------------------

CONFIRMED FROM THE CAMPAIGN FILE: `base genome2.mutation.point_q16 65535`,
ten times the shipped 6554, and `point_delta_q16 3277`.

  Step 1 - how often a birth touches one named edge locus.
    `mutate` fires point mutation once per birth with probability
    `point_q16 / 65536 = 65535/65536 = 0.999985` (structmut.rs:434).
    `point_mutate` then picks a haplotype (1/2), a chromosome (1/C), and one
    locus uniformly inside it (structmut.rs:528-533, :568).
    Measured from these snapshots rather than assumed from the founder,
    because duplication grows the denominator: Avar carries 7,609,038 loci
    over 166,271 organisms = **45.76 loci per genome**, in **1 chromosome
    per haplotype**, i.e. 22.88 loci per chromosome.

      P(a birth delivers a point mutation to one named edge locus)
        = 0.999985 * (1/2) * (1/1) * (1/22.88)
        = 1 / 45.76
        = 0.02185

  Step 2 - how many hits at that locus assemble the conjunction.
    Given a hit on an edge locus, the target is uniform on 7
    (`draw(8) % 7`, structmut.rs:631). Four of the seven matter.

    Naive coupon collector - four required coupons at 1/7 each, every hit
    succeeding and nothing ever reverting:
        E = 7 * (1 + 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/4) = 7 * 2.08333 = **14.58 hits**
    That is a floor and it is optimistic in three ways the operators make
    concrete:
      - target 1 TOGGLES the flag (structmut.rs:675), so the flag is set
        only after an odd number of hits;
      - target 2 RE-DRAWS the rule modulo 5 (structmut.rs:633, :676), so a
        hit sets a live rule with probability 4/5 and turns one off with
        probability 1/5;
      - target 3 adds a signed delta clamped at 0 from below
        (structmut.rs:677), so from eta = 0 only the 32,767 of 65,536
        upward draws move it, and a later downward draw can clamp it back;
      - target 4 adds a signed delta to one of four coefficients
        (structmut.rs:679-681); from zero, any of the 65,535 nonzero deltas
        works, so it is effectively sticky.

    Solving the exact 16-state absorbing chain over those four two-state
    conditions, driven at 1/7 per hit each with the transition
    probabilities above, gives the expected number of hits at one locus to
    have all four ON simultaneously:

        eta sticky once positive (optimistic)          **24.03 hits**
        eta falls back to 0 on one downward hit in 4     27.18 hits
        eta falls back to 0 on every downward step       29.66 hits

  Step 3 - in births.
        24.03 hits / 0.02185 hits per birth = **1,100 births in the direct
        line of descent of that one locus.**
    At the pessimistic end, 1,357.

  Step 4 - what 60,000 ticks actually delivered.
    The mean over the 30 Avar worlds of each world's `max_ancestry_depth`
    is **63.7 generations** - and that is a *maximum* per world, so the
    typical standing lineage is shallower and the shortfall below is a
    lower bound. A lineage of that depth receives

        63.7 * 0.02185 = **1.39 point mutations at any one locus**,
        i.e. 0.20 hits per target,

    against the ~24 needed. **The run is short by a factor of about 17.**
    To assemble the conjunction along one line of descent this campaign
    would have to have run roughly 1,100/64 ~ 17 times longer, or carry
    ~17x the mutational reach per generation.

  Step 5 - the population is large, and that is why the count is 9 and not
    0. Across the Avar arm 4,425,813 births produced 4,276,156 applied point
    mutations. Edge loci are 8.99% of all loci, so about **384,600 point
    mutations landed on edge loci, ~54,900 per target**. Those are spread
    over ~340,000 distinct edge loci in ~166,000 standing genomes and, with
    no gradient (section 3), they do not compose: an allele that acquired
    one condition has no fitness reason to spread while it waits for the
    next.

  CROSS-CHECK, and it is the strongest single result in this file. Fit the
  per-lineage hit rate from one marginal and predict the other three. The
  flag is ON iff an odd number of target-1 hits landed, so with mu hits per
  target per allele-lineage, P(flag) = (1 - exp(-2 mu))/2. The observed
  7.984% inverts to mu = 0.0868, i.e. 0.61 locus-hits per allele-lineage -
  about half the 1.39 the mean ancestry depth suggests, which is what one
  expects when the standing population is younger than the deepest lineage.
  With that single fitted number:

                          predicted    observed
      rule_id != 0          6.65%       6.179%
      eta > 0               4.16%       3.931%
      nonzero coefficient   8.31%       8.004%

  and the full conjunction under independence of the four indicators:

      Avar   684,370 * 0.07984 * 0.06179 * 0.03931 * 0.09073 = **12.0**
             observed **9**            Poisson(12.0): entirely ordinary
      Astat  375,318 * 0.07413 * 0.06822 * 0.04260 * 0.09104 = **7.4**
             observed **19**

  Avar's count is what four independent, unselected, drift-only conditions
  predict, to within Poisson noise. Astat's is 2.6x the independence
  expectation - and that is not evidence of selection in the *stationary*
  arm; it is pseudoreplication. Astat's 19 come from only 4 worlds, and 14
  of the 19 are in one world (seed 0x1786). Standing alleles are not
  independent draws: one assembly event followed by ordinary reproduction
  produces many identical copies. The same caution applies to Avar's 9,
  which come from 4 worlds with 6 in one of them.

  **So the honest count of independent assembly events is not 9 and 19. It
  is at most 4 and 4, in 30 worlds each.**

5. WHAT THIS CHANGES ABOUT THE PHASE 11 NULL
----------------------------------------------------------------

The findings file's explanation - a rate problem in `eta` and the
coefficients - is right in kind and understates the problem by two
mechanisms and one order of magnitude.

  - It names two genes. There are four conditions on the same locus, each
    behind a different one of seven mutation targets, plus a fifth for the
    two modulated rules that also needs a `Modulatory` node to exist on the
    same haplotype. The measured depth histogram is the shape of that: 76.6%
    of Avar edge alleles are at depth 0, 19.9% at depth 1, 3.3% at depth 2,
    0.22% at depth 3, and 0.0013% complete.

  - "The learn phase ran 1,109,373,897 times" counts 1,058,712,033 no-ops.
    The mechanism ran 50.7 million times, on 3,147 of 48,119 edges.

  - "the final mean absolute learned delta was 0 milli in all 30 Avar
    worlds" is true and is a truncation. 25 edges learned; the largest
    learned 229 milli; the total was 2.12 weight units spread over 48,119
    rows.

  - The reason is not only rate. It is that no intermediate is selectively
    visible and the flagged ones are selectively *negative*, so the rate is
    all there is: the search is an unassisted random walk over a
    four-condition conjunction at a single locus, and 64 generations of
    ancestry deliver 1.4 mutations to that locus against the ~24 needed.

WHAT A LATER REVISION WOULD HAVE TO CHANGE, stated as consequences of the
numbers above and not as a plan:

  - a founder that starts at depth 3 rather than depth 0 - a live rule, a
    positive eta and a nonzero coefficient, with only `EDGE_FLAG_PLASTIC`
    left to evolve - converts a four-step conjunction into a one-step
    toggle, and it is the only change that acts on all four conditions at
    once;
  - alternatively, make the flag the *only* gate and give the other three a
    nonzero founder value, which is the same thing said from the other side;
  - a horizon of ~1,100 generations at this locus rather than ~64;
  - or a mutation regime in which one point mutation can move more than one
    plasticity field. All four are changes to reachability. None of them is
    a change to what learning is worth, and this census cannot say anything
    about that.

6. WHAT THIS CENSUS DOES NOT ESTABLISH
----------------------------------------------------------------

  - It is descriptive. There is no threshold anywhere in it, and the
    comparisons between arms in section 2 are not tests. C11.1 and C11.2
    remain UNMET exactly as pre-registered and decided.
  - `full_conjunction` is an UPPER BOUND on what could have learned. It
    reads genes, not activations: an edge with all four conditions whose
    `pre` and `post` are zero every tick still learns nothing under rules
    1, 3 and 4, and any delta below half a Q16 unit is rounded away by
    `to_q16`. The learned-state count (25 in Avar) is the ground truth and
    is well below the 59 expressed-complete edges.
  - The counts are over the STANDING population at tick 60,000. An allele
    that assembled the conjunction at tick 30,000 and died out is invisible
    here, so this is a lower bound on how often the phenotype ever existed.
  - Alleles within a world are not independent. Section 4's pseudoreplication
    caveat is the load-bearing one: 9 alleles is at most 4 assembly events.
  - Nothing here measures whether learning would have been useful. It
    measures whether it was reachable, and separately whether the states on
    the way to it were visible to selection. A world in which the phenotype
    were common and still did nothing would produce the opposite of these
    numbers, and would be the finding this census was written to be able to
    report.
  - The waiting-time arithmetic in section 4 models the four conditions as
    independent two-state chains at one locus. It ignores linkage to the
    rest of the genome, meiotic recombination between the two alleles,
    duplication changing the locus count mid-run, and the fact that a
    lineage's mutations are not delivered at a constant rate. It is an
    order-of-magnitude statement and is reported as one.
  - `dt_ms`, the cost rate, the point rate and the delta width are this
    campaign's. Every number in section 3b and section 4 moves with them.
